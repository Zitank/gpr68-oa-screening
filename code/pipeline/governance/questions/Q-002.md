## Question (one sentence)

Does docking against the apo pH-6.8 GPR68 structure enrich known GPR68 modulators above chance, and is any enrichment specific to the proton-sensing pocket rather than the lipid-facing groove?

## Hypotheses

- H1 Pocket 1 enrichment beats chance (ROC-AUC bootstrap CI excludes 0.5).
- H2 Pocket 1 discriminates against pocket 2 for known modulators.
- H3 The pH 6.8 protomer scores known modulators at least as well as pH 7.4.

## Controls

Pocket 2 (centre 129.32, 131.62, 117.61), the lipid-facing groove, is the negative site. Property-matched decoys are the negative molecules. The 13 inactive compounds in PubChem AID 2060853 are the experimental check on those decoys. Pocket 1 (centre 145.67, 128.56, 99.49) is the site under test. Settings other than the box centre stay identical across the eight requested runs.

## Falsifiable form (what result would say no?)

A pocket-1 ROC-AUC bootstrap CI that includes 0.5, or pocket 1 indistinguishable from pocket 2, kills structure-based prioritisation on this structure. That is worth knowing before anyone buys compounds. H3 failing does not by itself answer H1 or H2.

## So what? If yes: ... / If no: ... / If null: ...

If yes: H1 and H2 both hold, so pocket-1 scores on this protocol are one allowed input to a later shortlist. The result stays exploratory. It does not show that a compound protects cartilage, and it does not show that ogerin binds in pocket 1.

If no: do not rank or purchase compounds by 8Z68 docking score. The eight runs and the enrichment benchmark have done their job by stopping that.

If null: the CI covers 0.5, or the two pockets' intervals overlap, or the only separation comes from decoys the real inactives expose as too easy. A point estimate above 0.5 is not enrichment.

## Cheapest discriminating experiment

First, the eight requested runs: ogerin (CID 56707820), MS48107 (CID 139030523), lorazepam (CID 3958) and ZINC32547799 (CID 39791182), each into pocket 1 and pocket 2, smina, same settings except the centre, logged with `run_logged()` and repeated with the same seed. That pilot can show a gross site difference. It cannot estimate a ROC-AUC, so it cannot confirm H1.

The experiment that can say no is the enrichment run: known modulators plus property-matched decoys, ROC-AUC, BEDROC and EF with bootstrap CIs, pocket 1 against pocket 2, then pH 6.8 against pH 7.4. Decoys are accepted only after the 13 real inactives are scored on the same protocol.

## Risks

The receptor is apo: there is no co-crystallised ligand to anchor the box. Box edge and exhaustiveness are assumptions A1 and A2, not measured constants. The Tanimoto cutoff for decoys is assumption A3. Protonation at pH 6.8 is assumption A5; HIS20 and HIS269 line pocket 1, so a wrong histidine state makes H3 meaningless. Ogerin is a PAM, so a poor pocket-1 score may mean the site is wrong rather than the engine is wrong. Pocket 2 as a lipid-facing negative control is an exploratory reading. Ogerin and ZINC32547799 differ by one substituent position, so the eight runs are a weak sample of chemical space. Smina reports a Vina-family score; GNINA CNN rescoring is not available on this machine. Defining "known modulator" by pooling EC50, IC50, pKb and activity-index rows would make the enrichment meaningless.

## Pilot plan (time box, compute budget, data, what is measured)

Priority is the eight runs, then the enrichment benchmark. Engine is smina on the Intel N100 (4 cores, no discrete GPU). Receptor is 8Z68 chain R. The eight-run pass measures one score per ligand–pocket pair and requires the second same-seed run to match the first. The enrichment pass measures ROC-AUC, BEDROC and EF at 1% and 5%, each with a bootstrap CI, plus seconds per ligand. pH 6.8 is the primary protomer and pH 7.4 the comparison. Seconds per ligand are written into `COMPUTE.md` only after they are measured.

## Kill criteria (written BEFORE the pilot)

Written before the runs. The bootstrap CI in these rules is a 95% interval. That level is an analysis choice fixed here so the stop rule is not chosen after seeing the scores.

1. If the pocket-1 ROC-AUC 95% bootstrap CI includes 0.5, kill structure-based prioritisation on this structure. Do not buy compounds on docking scores.
2. If the pocket-1 and pocket-2 ROC-AUC 95% bootstrap CIs overlap, treat the sites as indistinguishable and apply the same kill.
3. Either rule is sufficient. Both can fire.
4. The eight-run pilot cannot pass or fail rule 1 or rule 2. A score gap on four compounds is not enrichment.
5. If H3 fails (pH 6.8 scores known modulators worse than pH 7.4, 95% bootstrap CI on the paired difference excludes zero in that direction), drop the preference for the acidic protomer and say so. Do not use that failure to ignore rule 1 or rule 2, and do not use a passed H3 to rescue them.
6. If the 13 AID 2060853 inactives receive more favourable scores than the property-matched decoys and the 95% bootstrap CI on the median-score difference excludes zero, the decoys are too easy. Do not report EF, BEDROC or ROC-AUC from those decoys as evidence of enrichment.
7. Surviving all of the above still does not promote a claim.
